# Outreach - find a real need, make the offer

The outward-facing half of the Build track. `build-project` Door 1
finds the need; when the need is **outside the student's own life**
- a community hub, a school, a church team - this skill runs the
full campaign: seek the problem, reach out, discover what they
actually need, make the offer, and capture expressions of interest.

**The offer, in one line:** *"We're young builders. Tell us what is
hard to keep track of, plan, or share where you are - and we will
build you a small, simple tool for free, and hand it over as a
gift."*

This is one complete task with one complete kit. Work it in order.

## Safety first (read before anything else)

- A **parent, guardian or youth leader knows about every outreach
  step** and approves the target list before anything is sent.
- **Official channels only.** School outreach goes through a
  teacher, club leader or the front office - never cold-messaging
  students or staff privately. Church outreach goes through the
  leader who knows the student.
- **Never meet a stranger alone.** In-person meetings happen with a
  parent or leader present, in a public place or on the
  organisation's own premises during their hours.
- **Never share the student's home address, phone number or
  personal socials.** Use a family-monitored email address or the
  school-approved channel.
- **No personal data collection, ever.** No hidden costs, no
  sign-ups demanded from the people we serve, nothing public
  without a parent or leader sign-off.
- **One polite follow-up, maximum.** If the door is closed, thank
  them, log it, and move to the next target.

## The whole workflow at a glance

1. Make the **target list** (three to five places).
2. Write the **first message** (the offer + one question).
3. An adult checks it; send it through the proper channel.
4. Run the **discovery conversation** (the question set below).
5. **Qualify** the need against the five filters.
6. Write the **one-page brief**; get their yes.
7. Hand the qualified need to `build-project` as the project card.
8. Build, give it away (`handover`), log what happened, next round.

**The kit** (build once, reuse): a printable flyer, the first-message
template, the question sheet, the expressions-of-interest landing
page, and the outreach log.

## Step 1 - The target list

Where real needs live:

- **School** - a teacher, a club, the library, the front office,
  the canteen, the sports teams.
- **Church and its ministries** - welcome team, kids ministry,
  musicians, admin, the op shop if there is one.
- **Community hubs** - local library, community centre, sports
  club, aged care, food bank, scouts or guides, the neighbourhood
  house.

Pick **three to five** for the first round. For each one, write one
line: what might they struggle to keep track of, plan, or share?
That guess makes the first message specific instead of generic.
Don't boil the ocean - one round at a time.

## Step 2 - The first message (the offer + one question)

Short, honest, plain. About 120 words. A parent or leader reviews
it before it goes anywhere.

Sample:

> Hi [name / team], I'm [student], [age], from [school / church].
> I'm learning to build simple digital tools - small web apps, pages,
> dashboards - and I want to use that for our community. If there's
> something hard to keep track of, plan, or share where you are, I'd
> love to build you a small, simple tool **for free** and hand it over
> as a gift. No cost, nothing to install, no catch. One question:
> **what's the most annoying repetitive task in your week?**
> Thanks, [student] (my parent/leader is cc'd).

Channels: email, the school's office, a note dropped in at the
library desk, a chat after church. The flyer carries the same words
for noticeboards, with the landing-page link or a QR code.

## Step 3 - The discovery conversation (the question set)

This is the heart of it. The student asks about **problems, not
solutions**. Listen more than talk (aim for listening 70 per cent).
Write down exact phrases - "I retype the whole roster" is worth
more than any summary.

The questions:

1. What's the most annoying repetitive task in your week?
2. What do you keep track of on paper, or in your head?
3. What breaks whenever one person is away?
4. If you had a spare pair of hands for a week, what would they do?
5. What do you wish you had a simple tool for?
6. How do you cope with it now? (Show me the workaround.)
7. Who else has this problem?
8. What would "solved" look like to you?
9. What would make a new tool a bad fit here? (Be honest with me.)
10. If I brought back one small idea next week, who should see it?

Rules: don't pitch mid-conversation. Don't promise anything on the
spot - say *"let me take this away and come back with one small
idea."* Thank them for their time either way.

## Step 4 - Qualify the need (five filters)

A need is worth building when it passes all five:

- **Real** - a named person wants it and will use it.
- **Small** - the smallest version can work in two to six weeks of
  afternoons. If it's big, cut it down until it's small.
- **Buildable** - a local-first web app, a page, a dashboard, or a
  real-world build. No servers, no accounts, no money.
- **Safe and simple** - no personal data collection, no logins
  unless unavoidable, nothing public without sign-off.
- **Givable** - they will take it, use it, and it can be handed
  over cleanly (`handover`).

Fail = thank them, log it ("not now"), move on - or shrink the idea
and try the filters again. "Not now" is a normal, successful
outcome of outreach.

## Step 5 - The brief, then the yes

One page, written for them:

- The problem, **in their own words** (quote them).
- Who it's for.
- What "done" looks like - one sentence: *"It helps [who] to [what].
  Done means they use it once and it works."*
- What it won't do (the scope fence - honest and short).
- The give-away promise: free, theirs, handed over.

They confirm the brief; then it becomes the **project card** in
`build-project.md` and the build runs through `build-project`
(Door 2 and Door 3 as normal).

## Step 6 - The landing page (expressions of interest)

A single-page site that carries the offer to the next round of
people. The student **builds it themselves** (usually as a
`build-project` mini-build - learn by shipping it). It holds:

- **The offer** as the headline - one or two lines, the same words
  as the flyer.
- **What we can make** - three or four examples, including the
  student's own finished builds, and the build stories from
  `handover` - the stories are the proof.
- **How it works** - three steps: tell us what's hard / we scope
  one small thing / we build it free and hand it over.
- **Expressions of interest** - a short form or a clearly labelled
  email link, routed to the **family-monitored address**. Collect
  only what is needed: organisation name, what's hard, how to reach
  them, and a one-line consent note ("we'll reply to arrange a
  chat"). No analytics scripts, no third-party trackers.
- **The safety line** - "A young builder runs this with a parent or
  youth leader."

Local-first rules apply: one HTML file, no backend, hosted (with
parent/leader sign-off) at a stable link, or shared as a file. The
same page prints as a flyer: headline, three examples, QR code to
the page.

## Step 7 - Track everything (the log)

**`outreach-log.md`** in the profile directory - the record of the
campaign:

```markdown
# Outreach log

## Round 1 - October 2026

| Date | Target | Channel | Who spoke | What they said (their words) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-08 | Library | front desk note | the manager | "the holiday program bookings, it's all on paper" | qualifying |

Follow-ups (one, maximum):
- 2026-10-11 - Library - bring back one small idea

Outcomes:
- [not now / qualified / built / given - with a line each]
```

When a need qualifies, it goes to `build-project.md` (inbox or
active card). When it is given away, `handover` runs and the log
closes the loop with what the person said after they received it.

## The daily rhythm carries it

When a round is active, the rituals carry one line: the Morning
Minute names today's outreach step, the Check-In asks how it went,
the Planning Session slots the follow-up. The Build Round question
covers it too - "starting something new" can start with finding.

## Workshops

A workshop compresses this into blocks: morning = target list,
first messages, and discovery role-plays; afternoon = scope the
strongest need and build the landing page; close = send the first
message for real (with the leader's sign-off).

## Pitfalls

- **Leading with a solution.** Ask about problems; the tool comes
  later.
- **Promising on the spot.** "Let me scope it and come back."
- **Spamming.** Three to five targets, one follow-up each.
- **Collecting data.** Never - not on the page, not in person.
- **Going alone, or sharing personal contact details.** Adults
  present, official channels, family-monitored inbox.
- **Big ideas.** Shrink until it fits two to six weeks of
  afternoons. The smallest thing that helps is the whole design
  principle.
- **Treating "not now" as failure.** It is a normal outcome, and
  it is logged like everything else.
